Introduction — signals of a resilient sea

Unlike the land, in the deep dark ocean light does not travel far. In the deep water, light is a luxury only a few can enjoy and use because there are places in the ocean where light doesn’t even exist. Humans get their vision from light, however deep sea creatures like whales count on low-frequency sounds. Those harmonies are their version of light. These songs help whales find food for their survival, locate mates, and stay connected with their group.

For generations, we ignored the ocean’s warning signs, trusting it was too vast to fail.”
The Wall of Sound

Louder seas,
endangered lives.

The rising roar of human activity and its toll on whales.

The ocean is believed to be a silent, peaceful place. A few decades ago it was exactly what it was believed it was. However, as of now the peace of the ocean is destroyed by human activities. The sounds whales use to navigate their lives are disrupted by these activities. These low-frequency sounds get interrupted by massive cargo ships, sonar, and seismic waves, which makes the ocean dramatically louder. The noise pollution caused by such things puts the lives of whales in a survival crisis.

The Survival Crisis

Auditory chaos beneath the waves.

Auditory masking: the invisible threat to whale survival.

When the ocean becomes too loud, whales experience what scientists call “auditory masking”. According to NOAA Fisheries (2024), whales use sound as a biological sonar to find food. When the ocean becomes too loud, the distance at which a whale can communicate or find prey shrinks from hundreds of miles to just a few.

The research stated in the book Reducing vessel noise increases foraging in endangered killer whales, Rob Williams shows that female killer whales often forgo hunting entirely in noisy conditions to avoid wasting energy on unsuccessful attempts. While male whales try to push through, they waste significantly more energy searching for food with less success. The noise does not only make their lives miserable, it even starves them.

When we answer the call, the ocean answers back with life.
Clearing the Acoustic Fog

Slow down,
save the whales.

Lowering human noise gives whales a fighting chance.

We believe that to mitigate these issues, governments and other authorities who are responsible for such matters should implement mandatory slow zones in critical habitats. Implementing slow zones will reduce the noise underwater. By lowering human-created noise, it is possible to restore clearer communication lines for whales which will support their survival.

When we quiet the oceans, we give whales a chance to speak, to hunt, and to survive.
