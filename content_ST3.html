Introduction — Life Does Not Give Up Easily

The coral reefs are the New York city of the underwater world. They are built with millions of skyscrapers, the corals with billions of travelers, the fishes zooming past one another.

The dream city, lying below the deep blue waters, not seen by many, yet home to millions, fading by the passing second, but no one is questioning why?

Prior to turning bone white, the coral reefs illuminate a haunting blue or a purple shade of glow. To casual observer, at most it may look like a party, joyful and burst of bright colours. But scientists call this the “fluorescent scream”. It is the coral’s last scream for help to a produce a chemical sunscreen to protect themselves from the rising temperatures.

The city is not just fading

Its putting on its brightest, saddest smile before bidding the final goodbyes.

Problem — The Decline of Coral Reefs

Many of us saw the magic of the ocean through the lenses of the shows like In a Mermaid Tale or Finding Nemo. We told our parents about how amazing it would be to see such a world in real life. These reefs feel like a wonderstruck dream, mystical, colourful and impossible. However, with the way the world is heating up, that dream is becoming a fading memory.

The science, in simple terms

corals and their algae, zooxanthellae are best friends, coral provides a safe home, for which algae provide the food. But when the surrounding environment, water gets too hot (even by 1 or 2 degrees) the algae becomes stressed and produce toxins. To which the coral panics, and “evicts” its best friend, leaving the coral transparent and white.

"Bleaching is like a fever. A small temperature spike for us is a bed-rest day; for a coral, it’s a fight for life." — Inspired by marine biologist Gareth Phillips.

Why It Matters — The Consequences of a World Without Reefs

Why should we care about this? Though coral less than 1% of the ocean bed, it is the home for 25% of the marine species. If we lose the “skyscrapers”, the “residents” have nowhere to go. An empty reef leads to an empty ocean.

The coral reefs protect the coastal lines

without it the ocean will directly slam in to the coastal towns. There is a huge difference between a splash and a destructive flood.

Furthermore, these reefs are our “underwater shields”. They break the power of waves before they reach the shore. Without them, the ocean will slam directly into the coastal towns; there is a massive difference between a gentle splash and a destructive flood. Beyond protection, corals are our “Global Pharmacy”. They are essential for research into cancer, HIV / AIDS, brain mapping. If the corals die, our hope for these medical breakthroughs may die beside them.

Impact — How Bleaching is Killing the Great Barrier Reef

A healthy reef is a noisy place, filled with the snaps, and grunts of life. A bleached reef, however, is like a dead man, no sound what so ever.Since 2016, the Great Barrier Reef has suffered six mass bleachings. The record breaking heatwaves of 2023-2025 were the most widespread in history, transforming this “fever” into a global pandemic.

While cold-stress events, like 2010 Florida Keys event, can also cause bleaching the heat of the last few years is the primary killer.

Recommendations — What We Can Do to Restore Our Coral Reefs

We must right the wrong. It is time to look beyond the “not littering” and think about the bigger picture, the “big stuff”, act on the impossible. We must shift toward renewable energy and support reef restoration.

There is hope in the lab.

Scientists are now breading "heat tough" corals, that can survive on warmer waters. By mapping the genes of the survivors, they are trying to “renovate” our underwater cities to withstand the new climate. If we don’t take chance on the “impossible” now, we will be the generation that watched a wonder of the world turn in to a ghost town.
